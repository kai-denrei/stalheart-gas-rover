# FEIDS-26 mobile defense

Each race deploys two FEIDS-26 capsules / four MX-06 Ridgebacks beside its finish area when the first racer clears 65% of the gates. One deployment per race; restart resets it. The Stage Maker also uses the authored finish position. If an authored stage leaves no safe apron, deployment is omitted.

The landing site reserves ground clearance, the full overhead descent corridor, future sinkhole footprints and the track approach. The original 28-second procedural asset sequence is retained: splits at 6.2 / 6.65 seconds and touchdowns at 13.00 / 13.43 / 14.00 / 19.23 seconds, with folded cargo, separating shell quarters, heated shields, flame ribbons, ventral braking jets, hydraulic compression, dust and ground cracks. Camera control stays with the racer.

Two seconds after each touchdown, the mech can walk toward nearby live enemies. It turns its body toward targets, avoids structures, the race surface, holes, racers and other mechs, and remains within 95 metres of its landing position. Original alternating-tripod IK and telescopic hydraulics run from distance travelled. Each mech has two independent six-socket Quiver pods, a forward-only rotor gun and an articulated Lancer. Quivers use the existing Stalheart unlit ejection/drop/ignition/apex/dive flight; rounds, heat, spin-up, locks, beams, damage and explosions share the city-defense simulation. Rotor rounds follow their physical muzzle direction and require body alignment. Mechs are friendly, persistent support units; this version does not add mech health or destruction.

Audio uses the three supplied MP3s, copied unchanged. Each capsule re-entry track ends at its split, with a small explosion sound at opening. Each ten-second mech recording starts exactly four seconds before its own touchdown. Rubble starts 60 ms after touchdown. These voices are scheduled separately from the capped combat sound pool, attenuate with listener distance, and seek to the correct recording offset after pause/resume. The supplied recording's broad impact transient spans roughly 3.7–4.2 seconds; the visual hit is aligned to 4.0 seconds.

Assets are pinned at `jelaludo/SentryTowers_A6` commit `1b9a85e34bd2b1c3738d3a66ce3c0b1ff24c7e26`, with original runtime/timeline modules under `upstream/a6/` and hashes in `assets.lock.json`. Models by jelaludo. No main-game files were modified. `src/feids-effects.js` adapts the source Heavy Rain trajectories into two bounded instanced sprite batches (180 flame / 168 dust particles). Articulated game meshes remain active during movement and combat; distant idle units use the static one-draw LOD.

Validation: `node --test test/feids.test.mjs`, `npm run test:feids` and `npm run profile:feids`. Browser acceptance checks both courses, all four impacts, walking, all weapon types firing at live targets, audio scheduling and seek offsets, clean reset and console errors. A local 1440 × 913 / DPR 1 live battle sample with 478 aliens and four active mechs measured 60 FPS, 2.72 ms simulation, 3.89 ms render submission and 3.50 ms GPU. This is a local sample, not a phone performance claim.

All 205 unit tests, pinned asset/module checks, both complete race browser runs, FEIDS acceptance on both courses and high-altitude entry/split visual checks passed. The Last Leap completed all three physical podium claims at 128.08 simulated seconds; Great Divide at 271.67 seconds.
